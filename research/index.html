research

Main research axes, with key publications, code and projects.

My research focuses on control theory and its applications to life sciences. I resort to theoretical and numerical optimal control approaches to predict biological phenomena, such as regulatory mechanisms in bacterial cells or transitions in gene regulatory networks, and to develop control strategies for industrial and biotechnological processes, such as wine fermentation. My work is organized around the three axes below.

Optimal resource allocation in microbial growth

Bacteria continuously redistribute their protein synthesis capacity among metabolic enzymes, ribosomes and other cellular functions. Using coarse-grained self-replicator models, I formulate this allocation as an optimal control problem and study its solutions analytically and numerically.

This framework helps explain natural behaviours such as growth laws, diauxic growth on multiple substrates or the response to heat shock, and suggests synthetic strategies to maximize the production of metabolites of interest in batch, fed-batch and continuous bioreactors.

Control of gene regulatory networks

Gene regulatory networks can be approximated by piecewise-linear models, which capture switch-like behaviours such as bistability and oscillations while remaining amenable to mathematical analysis.

We study optimal control problems on these hybrid systems, such as time-optimal and L¹-optimal state transitions, to understand how an external inducer can drive a network from one regime to another: switching a bistable toggle switch, or changing the oscillation regime of the p53–Mdm2 network. The results characterize the structure of optimal controls, the role of separatrices and abnormal extremals, and lead to feedback control laws.

Modelling and control of wine fermentation

Wine fermentation is a bioprocess where control can improve product quality while reducing energy consumption. Together with colleagues at MISTEA and the SPO and Pech Rouge units, we develop mechanistic models of yeast metabolism and aroma synthesis, such as the production of isoamyl acetate.

Building on these models, we work on the multiobjective optimization of temperature profiles that balance aroma production and energy consumption, with the goal of real-time control of industrial fermentations.
